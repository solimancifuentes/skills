# Sampling and evaluation

Read the sampling portion when selecting a collection or answering how much writing is needed. Read evaluation when testing an existing profile or assessing how well a new draft reproduces it.

## Sampling is a design choice, not a word-count gate

The following ranges are editorial planning heuristics for assembling a starting collection. They are not experimentally validated word thresholds, an established population average, or promises of performance:

| Intended work | Possible starting collection |
| --- | --- |
| First profile and provisional imitation | 1,000–3,000 words |
| Detailed profile for one main format | 5,000–10,000 words |
| Several formats, audiences, or tones | 15,000–30,000 words, grouped by context |

Around 10,000 words across 10–20 medium-length pieces is one practical starting plan, not a requirement. Short-form writing may need many smaller independent examples without approaching this total. A single long text offers less evidence about variation across occasions than a varied collection, even when their word counts match. Longer or complex forms may need complete works to reveal structure beyond paragraph-level patterns.

Start with available material. Distinguish insufficient evidence for a particular claim from inability to do any useful work. If more evidence is needed, identify the missing context: for example, an actual disagreement, a long explanation, or a conversational reply. More words of the same kind may not address the gap.

### Selection priorities

- Use authentic work substantially authored by the target person. Record known collaboration, editing, translation, or AI assistance rather than silently treating it as solo writing.
- Match the intended genre and audience while varying subjects enough to expose choices that survive topic changes.
- Include ordinary work and characteristic exceptions, not only unusually polished favorites.
- Preserve complete pieces when openings, development, and endings matter. Remove duplicates and repeated boilerplate from pattern counts.
- Keep materially different languages, periods, and registers identifiable. Prefer the period the user wants to reproduce.
- Respect context and privacy: use the supplied or authorized sources rather than searching unrelated personal accounts for more samples.

## Distinguish extraction, generation, and validation

Recognizing a feature does not prove an AI can reproduce the combination naturally. Producing one convincing passage does not establish reliable performance across topics, formats, or long documents. Authorship-attribution accuracy, automated style similarity, and human judgments measure different things.

A 2025 study tested personal-style imitation across news, email, forums, and blogs. In its follow-up experiments, increasing demonstrations from 2 to 10 yielded limited gains under its automated metrics. Its scope was within-genre English writing, using the models tested at that time, without large-scale human evaluation. It does not validate the word-count estimates above or establish a universal ceiling for later models or other workflows.

Source: [Wang et al., Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet, Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.532/). [Accessible paper text, sections 6 and Limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1). Recheck research only when the task needs current comparative or quantitative claims; a routine profile does not require a literature search.

## Proportionate evaluation

For an ordinary draft, a focused internal comparison is sufficient to deliver provisionally. For a claim of strong reproduction, choose a small evaluation that matches the intended use:

1. **Reserve authentic pieces when available.** Keep them out of profile extraction and example selection. For a very small collection, explain that a clean held-out evaluation is unavailable rather than disguising reuse as independence.
2. **Test new content.** Use a fresh topic or task in the target format. A content brief should specify the facts and intended message without leaking the original author's wording.
3. **Compare the right things.** Assess supported defining traits, naturalness, context fit, and the integrity of the new content. Confirm that recognizable resemblance does not depend on copied phrases or transferred personal anecdotes.
4. **Obtain author feedback when feasible.** Ask which specific choices feel unlike them and why. Feedback from an actual writer or knowledgeable reader is stronger evidence of acceptance than an AI declaring its own output convincing.
5. **Correct the cause.** Revise the draft if it missed an established trait; revise the profile if the trait was misread or too broad. Add samples only when a demonstrated gap calls for them.

If a comparison piece was already used during extraction, call the result an in-sample comparison. If a held-out example is later used to revise the profile, it is no longer held out for the next evaluation. Self-review or another AI's review is not author validation.

### Useful assessment dimensions

- **Content fidelity:** Facts, intended meaning, stance requested for the assignment, and protected quotations remain correct.
- **Structural fidelity:** Openings, development, emphasis, transitions, and endings reflect supported patterns.
- **Language fidelity:** Diction, grammar, syntax, rhythm, and mechanics resemble the relevant context without caricature.
- **Relational fidelity:** Tone, certainty, politeness, and reader relationship fit the intended situation.
- **Independence:** The draft does not recycle distinctive source passages, fictionalize personal experience, or rely on the original topic to sound familiar.
- **Consistency and naturalness:** Defining choices persist across the draft without exaggerated ticks or artificial uniformity.

Use qualitative judgments with examples unless the user requests a defined scoring rubric. Do not present unsupported percentages, automated authorship scores, or AI-detection results as proof of authenticity.

## Stopping and reporting

Deliver once the requested artifact and appropriate checks are complete. Stronger confidence comes from consistent performance on several relevant new tasks and, where available, author feedback requiring only light stylistic edits. Word count alone cannot establish this.

State whether the profile is provisional, internally checked, tested on held-out material, or reviewed by the author. Identify the contexts actually evaluated. If performance stalls, report the specific remaining mismatch and the smallest useful next step rather than repeatedly collecting more text or claiming a perfect match.
