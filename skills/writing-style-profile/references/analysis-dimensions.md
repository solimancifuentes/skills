# Analysis dimensions

Use this map to identify consequential patterns in supplied writing. Select dimensions relevant to the requested output. A comprehensive analysis may account for all relevant categories, but ordinary profiles should emphasize the traits that distinguish this author and improve drafting.

## Coverage map

### Communicative context

1. **Purpose, audience, and situation:** Intended function and response; reader knowledge; occasion and stakes; genre, medium, and constraints. Record these as context rather than assuming they are permanent author traits.
2. **Content selection:** Recurring subjects and themes; breadth and depth; selection and omission; abstraction, detail, originality, and contextualization. Separate topic preferences from transferable language patterns.
3. **Voice and persona:** Perceived speaker, authorial visibility, basis of authority, self-positioning, disclosure, individuality, and self-awareness. Describe the textual presentation rather than diagnosing the person.
4. **Tone:** Warmth, seriousness, emotional character and intensity, outlook, evaluation, reverence, curiosity, urgency, restraint, and changes in attitude.
5. **Reader relationship and pragmatics:** Address, proximity, hierarchy, alignment, directness, explicitness, presupposition, implication, speech acts, politeness, reader autonomy, and anticipated objections.
6. **Register and linguistic variety:** Formality, conversationality, literary or professional register, regional/social/historical variety, multilingual practice, audience accommodation, and language transfer.

### Language choices

7. **Diction and word formation:** Vocabulary range and repetition, familiarity, precision, concreteness, connotation, verbs, modifiers, idioms, stock phrases, lexical invention, compounding, derivation, nominalization, and shortening.
8. **Grammar:** Agreement, tense, aspect, mood, modality, grammatical voice, person and number, pronoun forms, inflection, determiners, quantification, countability, comparison, negation, complements, and relevant language-specific categories.
9. **Syntax:** Sentence length and type; clause composition, coordination and subordination; word order, branching, delayed or early completion, embedding, dependency distance, modifier scope, nonfinite forms, apposition, parallelism, fragments, omission, and focus constructions.
10. **Punctuation:** Sentence boundaries, commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, parentheses, brackets, quotations, apostrophes, ellipses, hyphens, slashes, density, and expressive departures.
11. **Orthography and notation:** Spelling convention, capitalization, script, diacritics, word boundaries, abbreviations, numbers, dates, units, specialized notation, and deliberate respelling.
12. **Rhythm and sound:** Cadence, stress, unit-length variation, pauses, verbal tempo, sound texture, alliteration, assonance, consonance, rhyme, onomatopoeia, and read-aloud flow.

### Discourse and thought

13. **Overall organization:** Opening, central-point placement, ordering principle, hierarchy, proportion, modularity, framing, digression, recurrence, ending, and title function.
14. **Paragraphing and information flow:** Paragraph size, unity, development and breaks; linguistic cohesion, conceptual coherence, reference tracking, given-to-new progression, topic continuity, focus, signposting, and reiteration.
15. **Explanation and instruction:** Definitions, examples, comparisons, analogies, mechanisms, scaffolding, procedures, exceptions, summaries, synthesis, actionability, and demonstrations.
16. **Rhetorical technique:** Metaphor, simile, personification, metonymy, synecdoche, symbolism, exaggeration, understatement, contrast, paradox, rhetorical questions, repetition, accumulation, reversal, allusion, double meaning, and defamiliarization. Record effects and frequency rather than collecting device names.
17. **Reasoning and persuasion:** Claim types, inferential method, premises and warrants, reasoning transparency, consistency, causal discipline, counterarguments, concessions, tradeoffs, criteria, appeals, and argumentative fairness.
18. **Evidence and certainty:** Evidence types and quality, traceability, attribution, quotation and paraphrase, fact/inference distinctions, calibrated confidence, evidential wording, generalization, qualification, numerical treatment, and acknowledged uncertainty.

### Narrative and literary modes

19. **Description and imagery:** Sensory emphasis, selected details, density, spatial orientation, movement, observational stance, showing and telling, recurring image families, and atmosphere.
20. **Narrative perspective:** Who tells, who perceives, narrator participation, access to knowledge, reliability, distance, hindsight, commentary, and viewpoint shifts.
21. **Story construction:** Event selection, causality, chronology, story time relative to telling time, retelling frequency, scene and summary, conflict, stakes, suspense, revelation, arcs, worldbuilding, payoff, and closure.
22. **Character, dialogue, and thought:** Complexity, characterization methods, motivations, differentiation, relationships, conversational naturalism and compression, subtext, turn-taking, speech framing, and presentation of thought.
23. **Humor, irony, and play:** Mode, mechanism, target, social function, timing, delivery, frequency, ironic distance, and self-directed humor. Irony need not be comic.
24. **Genre-specific form:** Poetry's lineation, stanzas, meter and formal constraints; dramatic staging; academic and journalistic conventions; correspondence openings and closings; business, technical, and experimental structures. Select only the applicable forms.

### Presentation, interpretation, and practice

25. **Visual presentation:** Typography, headings, lists and tables, whitespace, alignment, page composition, emphasis, text-image relations, peripheral text, and visual accessibility. Use the actual rendered source when needed; plain text may not preserve these features.
26. **Digital interaction:** Links, platform adaptation, chunking, navigation, branching, interface messages, discoverability, mentions/tags/emojis, conversation management, revision visibility, and assistive access.
27. **Values and representation:** Textual framing, stated values, agency and responsibility, labels, cultural assumptions, included perspectives, stereotyping, humanization, discretion, persuasive ethics, and treatment of harm. Do not infer enduring private beliefs from a narrow sample.
28. **Editorial finish:** Grammar relative to the chosen variety; mechanical and terminological consistency; factual/narrative continuity; reference integrity; house style; recurring slips versus intentional departures. Preserve style without automatically reproducing consequential errors.
29. **Reader effects:** Clarity, readability, cognitive load, economy, interpretive openness, engagement, emotional resonance, credibility, memorability, distinctiveness, usability, and audience fit. Predictions of reader response remain interpretations until assessed with readers.
30. **Writing practice:** Planning, research, drafting, revision, proofreading, feedback, collaboration, tools, versioning, and development over time. These usually require process evidence or author testimony; finished prose alone is insufficient.

## Distinctions that prevent weak profiles

- **Voice vs. tone:** Perceived speaker identity versus attitude in a particular passage.
- **Grammar vs. syntax:** Grammatical forms and relationships versus arrangement and sentence architecture; avoid listing the same observation twice.
- **Register vs. dialect:** Choices associated with the situation versus a linguistic community. Neither is simply correctness.
- **Cohesion vs. coherence:** Visible linguistic links versus meaningful conceptual connections.
- **Narrator vs. focalizer vs. author:** Who tells, whose perceptions organize the account, and the real person who wrote it.
- **Observed frequency vs. universal rule:** Recurrence supports a preference; it does not establish an absolute prohibition or requirement.
- **Mechanism vs. outcome:** Short paragraphs are observable; effortless reading is an interpretation that depends on the audience.
- **Style vs. identity or truth:** Stylistic resemblance does not establish authorship, beliefs, experiences, or factual accuracy.

## Turn observations into reusable guidance

For each defining trait, retain enough of the following to make it usable:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Behavior | A concrete choice rather than an adjective such as "authentic" or "engaging." |
| Evidence | A short exact excerpt or inspectable pattern, with source labels and locations when available. |
| Scope | The relevant format, audience, language, purpose, or period. |
| Support | Strong, moderate, or tentative, with the reason; avoid fabricated statistical confidence. |
| Application | What to do in a new draft and when to do it. |
| Exceptions | Where the author uses a different choice or the evidence conflicts. |

Synthetic example: "In samples A and C, explanations open with a concrete consequence before introducing terminology. For introductory explanations, lead with the consequence and define the term afterward. Sample B is a technical reference and does not follow this sequence." This is more useful than "writes clearly and practically."

If the user requests exhaustive coverage, mark relevant dimensions as supported, tentative, unobserved, or not applicable. Do not manufacture a finding to fill each category.
