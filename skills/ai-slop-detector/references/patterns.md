# Pattern catalog

Stable IDs preserve the 57 aspects from the source synthesis. Their assigned scoring dimensions live in `rubric.json`. A name is a cue to inspect, not proof of a defect. Explain the actual reader cost and context before assigning impact. Reviewed patterns retain a maximum supported local impact of zero when no supported finding is established; tentative concerns remain visible. Distinguish not applicable, not assessable, and not checked as specified in the scoring contract. Multiple IDs can describe one underlying finding without multiplying its penalty.

## Substance and rhetorical habits

| ID | Pattern | What supports a finding; what to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | Portable generic statement | Content could move to unrelated subjects without losing meaning. Preserve useful general principles and necessary shared instructions. |
| P02 | Benefit without explanation | An asserted benefit supplies neither mechanism nor relevant detail. Brief summaries may link to supporting explanations rather than repeat them. |
| P03 | Inflated significance | Importance exceeds the support shown. Real consequences can justify strong language; do not equate enthusiasm with error. |
| P04 | Commentary posing as analysis | A trailing interpretation repeats significance instead of establishing consequence. An explanatory participial clause is not inherently filler. |
| P05 | Unidentifiable authority | Unnamed research, experts, or consensus carry argumentative weight without checkable support. Mark support missing, not necessarily false. |
| P06 | Throat-clearing | Announcing honesty, directness, or a point delays it without social or narrative purpose. Preserve purposeful conversational setup. |
| P07 | Stock context opening | A generic claim about modern life or rapid change delays the specific subject. Genuine historical context can matter. |
| P08 | Empty emphasis | Commands to notice, pause, or recognize importance substitute for support. Instructions to observe a real result are useful. |
| P09 | Narrating the document | Repeated previews and self-commentary displace content. A roadmap can help a long or complex document. |
| P10 | Performed intimacy | Generic sincerity or reassurance supplies no situation-specific help. Preserve warranted empathy, personal voice, and actual commitments. |
| P11 | Forced binary contrast | Negation/revelation creates a false opposition or unnecessary pivot. Preserve real alternatives, corrections, tradeoffs, and distinctions. |
| P12 | Negative-list buildup | Rejecting several identities delays the answer without resolving likely confusion. Explicit exclusions can define scope accurately. |
| P13 | Manufactured transformation | A dramatic before/after identity change outruns the actual difference. Evidence-supported change is legitimate. |
| P14 | Staged question and answer | A self-answered question manufactures suspense. Preserve real FAQs, teaching questions, and useful inquiry. |
| P15 | Unsupported generalization | Universal or sweeping scope exceeds supplied support. A genuinely universal rule or bounded exhaustive result can be stated plainly. |
| P16 | Unearned insider status | The writer claims others overlook something without demonstrating that claim. A documented knowledge gap is different. |
| P17 | Dramatic colon reveal | The colon stages a trivial disclosure rather than serving grammar. Lists, definitions, examples, and useful labels are valid. |
| P18 | Dramatic fragments | Repeated clipped pieces imitate gravity without developing thought. Preserve clear, purposeful fragments and authentic cadence. |
| P19 | Synonym cycling | Unnecessary label changes obscure a stable referent. Distinct entities or technical concepts may require distinct words. |
| P20 | Engineered profundity | A slogan or final flourish replaces precision with apparent depth. A memorable line is not defective merely because it is quotable. |

## Language, rhythm, and clarity

| ID | Pattern | What supports a finding; what to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P21 | Inflated vocabulary | Formal or fashionable language buys no precision. Terms such as robust, leverage, or modality can be literal technical terms. |
| P22 | Abstract jargon | Technical-sounding abstractions hide a referent or action. Keep established terms, identifiers, and concepts the audience needs. |
| P23 | Padded verb phrase | Elaborate phrasing obscures a simple action or relationship. Prefer precise verbs, not automatic substitutions. |
| P24 | Empty modifier | Intensity or sincerity words add no information. Preserve modifiers that express timing, degree, contrast, limits, or voice. |
| P25 | Stacked hedging | Several qualifications repeat the same uncertainty. Preserve the minimum language that accurately reflects the evidence. |
| P26 | False range | Unrelated examples masquerade as endpoints of a scale. Legitimate ranges and explicitly illustrative lists are fine. |
| P27 | Forced triad | List length forces redundant or artificial categories. Three real items require no penalty. |
| P28 | Mechanical rhythm | Repeated sentence openings, lengths, or paragraph formulas obstruct natural development. Required parallelism and accessible structure can help. |
| P29 | Overloaded sentence | Clause or noun density forces rereading or obscures conditions. Keep a longer sentence when it carries one coherent relationship. |
| P30 | Excessive compression | Missing articles, verbs, explanations, or excessive symbols force decoding. Preserve code, mathematical notation, and established shorthand where appropriate. |
| P31 | Ambiguous reference | Pronouns, modifiers, or conjunctions permit materially different readings. Identify the competing readings rather than merely preferring a rewrite. |
| P32 | Reader-task mismatch | Instructions, reference, explanation, and persuasion are mixed in a way that obstructs the reader's goal. Genre mixing alone is not a defect. |
| P33 | Sterile explanation | Correct prose omits the example, distinction, or judgment needed for understanding. Reference material need not supply personality or an opinion. |
| P34 | Hidden responsibility | Known responsibility matters but the phrasing conceals it. Passive voice is valid when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. |
| P35 | Misleading personification | Abstract agency obscures the real mechanism or decision. A compiler can validate, an app can send, and ordinary figurative shorthand can be clear. |
| P36 | Detached generalization | Broad claims about people's thoughts or behavior lack appropriate grounding. Do not require second person or invent lived experience. |

## Presentation and reader relationship

| ID | Pattern | What supports a finding; what to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P37 | Decorative formatting | Formatting supplies apparent importance without navigation or comprehension value. No inherent penalty for headings, bold, emoji, or quotation style. |
| P38 | Redundant labels | A heading or lead-in immediately repeats the same content. Useful labels summarize and then add detail. |
| P39 | Punctuation as performance | Habitual punctuation manufactures the same dramatic cadence. No automatic penalty for dashes, colons, parentheses, or curly quotes. |
| P40 | Reflexive praise or agreement | Flattery or endorsement replaces evaluation of the actual premise. Courteous acknowledgment can serve the interaction. |
| P41 | Chatbot residue | Generic acknowledgments, offers, or sign-offs remain where the intended artifact should stand alone. An actual chat may need interaction language. |
| P42 | Teaching theater | Forced reveals, quizzes, pauses, or importance cues interrupt understanding. Real pedagogical exercises and requested pacing are legitimate. |
| P43 | Description without explanation | An inventory of parts or facts fails to answer the requested mechanism or problem. Do not penalize an inventory requested as an inventory. |
| P44 | Process dump | Internal logs, file tours, or workflow history obscure the result. Preserve an audit trail when the task requires one. |
| P45 | Redundant conclusion | A recap repeats nearby content without useful synthesis, decision, or action. Summaries can help long, technical, or instructional documents. |
| P46 | Empty optimistic ending | Unsupported future promises replace a concrete finish. A supported forecast or explicit aspiration can be appropriate. |
| P47 | Redundant interpretation | The text re-explains an already clear point without advancing the argument. Preserve disambiguation or translation that the audience needs. |

## Comparison- and evidence-dependent checks

| ID | Pattern | Required evidence and guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P48 | Voice flattening | Original or relevant voice reference plus target demonstrates loss of characteristic diction, pacing, or perspective. A polished standalone text cannot establish a change. |
| P49 | Unnecessary professionalization | Original or voice reference demonstrates needless sanitizing of useful character. Adaptation to a requested audience can justify the change. |
| P50 | Over-editing for consistency | Original and target show needless structural or sentence changes. Do not infer the editing process from a single draft. |
| P51 | Invented specificity | Original and target show newly added, unsupported specifics. An addition is not proven false; label it unsupported unless supplied evidence establishes fabrication. |
| P52 | Meaning lost in editing | Original and target demonstrate removal or alteration of relevant facts, scope, conditions, or connections. Quote both sides. |
| P53 | False certainty | The text or supplied record shows a claim expressed more strongly than its evidence permits. Mere confidence is not a finding. |
| P54 | Plausible story treated as established | Contextual evidence shows motive, causation, or history presented as fact beyond what the record establishes. With no record, identify a verification need rather than assert fabrication. |
| P55 | Selective coherence | Available context contains material conflicts, alternatives, or gaps omitted by the account. No source record means omission cannot be established. |
| P56 | Citation mismatch | Read the actual cited source and quote the support/mismatch. A citation's presence or suspicious appearance is insufficient. |
| P57 | Agreement replacing investigation | Original question and contextual investigation record show material alternatives ignored in favor of the suggested answer. Do not infer a research process from agreement alone. |

When contextual checks cannot be performed, record the missing material. Do not replace these tests with intuition about how AI sounds. User-specific bans belong in a separate style-compliance note unless they cause one of the defects above.
