# Evidence and scope

This appendix separates inspectable source observations from editorial design choices. It is a maintenance reference; ordinary drafting does not require reading the source articles. The observations describe writing techniques, not factual claims to carry into new work.

## Supported observations

**Establish the subject early.** The Projects announcement begins with the release and its intended uses. The Grab story foregrounds an organizational consequence before developing role-specific examples. These openings support early orientation, without requiring every piece to use the same formula. See the opening paragraphs of [Projects](https://cursor.com/blog/projects) and [Grab](https://cursor.com/blog/grab).

**Explain through dependencies.** The Projects announcement connects a coordinator's delegated work to its claimed responsiveness. The Git essay traces storage constraints through design alternatives and their consequences. Both illustrate explanations that make the reasoning visible; adopting that method does not endorse the underlying technical claims. See the coordinator section of [Projects](https://cursor.com/blog/projects) and the early storage sections of [Git at any scale](https://cursor.com/blog/git-at-any-scale).

**Connect abstractions to work.** The Grab story follows adoption figures with specific activities performed by designers, engineers, and other teams. Its account of debugging names concrete starting points and ways to check a result. This supports precise objects and observable actions. Technical vocabulary and numerical density depend on the subject. See the opening and design section of [Grab](https://cursor.com/blog/grab).

**Use rhythm and character locally.** The Git essay alternates developed explanations with brief judgments, rhetorical questions, personal recollections, and sarcastic asides. Its account of early filesystem approaches ends a longer setup with an abrupt verdict. These choices are clear within that essay; three sources do not establish their frequency across the original collection. Its personal history belongs to its narrator. See the opening and filesystem discussion in [Git at any scale](https://cursor.com/blog/git-at-any-scale).

## Editorial design choices and limits

The direct, concrete, conversational default synthesizes these observations. Grounded confidence and locally qualified uncertainty are editorial preferences, not universal habits established by these sources. Preserve evidence limits even when an example uses promotional certainty.

General nonfiction, personal commentary, humor, forceful opinions, and reflections without a practical lesson are deliberate extensions beyond the sources' public technology contexts. They are available registers, not required features of every piece. Pragmatic Builder names this editorial voice; it does not attribute new writing to any source author.

English is evidenced. Other languages and unrelated genres remain provisional transfers, with the assignment and language's natural usage taking precedence. The sources do not establish cross-model reliability, author-reviewed fidelity, or a percentage match. Source comparison supports bounded observations; behavioral trials can test decisions but cannot establish authentic authorship.
