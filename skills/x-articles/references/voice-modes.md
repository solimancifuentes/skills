# Optional voice modes

X Articles accepts any requested voice. These summaries support explicit requests for the English-language Potato modes without requiring another skill. Preserve another selected voice or the supplied draft's voice when no Potato mode is requested. A supplied voice profile adds detail under the applicable instruction hierarchy.

When Potato is requested without a mode, use sweet-potato. Use raw-potato when named or when the Potato request clearly calls for direct engineer-to-engineer treatment. An explicit mode choice wins over audience inference. A switch applies to the current piece, not future assignments.

## Sweet-potato

Write as an engineer explaining something to users or a broader audience. Lead with what it means for the reader, explain the mechanism in familiar language, and connect causes to consequences. Introduce unfamiliar terms without avoiding necessary technical detail. Treat the reader as capable, handle real objections patiently, and give enough context to follow the explanation. Do not invent the reader's feelings, experience or circumstances, or add canned reassurance.

Potato's original presentation is simple connected paragraphs with lowercase ordinary prose and the pronoun "i". X Articles adapts the capitalization to sentence case by default; retain the original lowercase presentation when explicitly required. Use no body headings, lists, tables, emphasis, emoji or decorative symbols unless the assignment requests a presentation adaptation. Prefer periods and commas; avoid em dashes, en dashes and hyphen-as-dash substitutes. Preserve protected quotations, proper names, acronyms, identifiers, URLs, units and other exact notation when changing them would break meaning. Expand where understanding requires it, then stop.

## Raw-potato

Write as an engineer addressing engineering peers. Lead with the result, decision, diagnosis or constraint. Name the component, operation, failure condition and consequence precisely. Preserve specifications, measurements, versions, conditions and uncertainty when supplied. Separate observations from estimates, hypotheses and predictions; explain what the evidence supports without inventing precision or treating correlation as causation.

Use conventional capitalization, sentence-case headings and compact, complete paragraphs. Use tables for comparable specifications, lists for steps or criteria, and code or equations when useful. Keep causal links and material caveats while removing preambles and repeated conclusions. Concision removes waste without imposing an arbitrary length. Directness does not require hostility, sarcasm or a performance of expertise.

## Combine voice and article presentation

Use sentence-case titles and headings with normal sentence capitalization in body prose by default, including both voice adaptations. A request for strict sweet-potato or the original lowercase presentation instead keeps lowercase, paragraph-only prose. A generic request for an X Article does not override that explicit requirement. Keep a required article title distinct from body formatting and apply the requested casing to it.

When the assignment explicitly requests sweet-potato's explanatory voice with headings, tables or conventional capitalization, retain those elements and apply the voice through explanation, reader relationship and tone. This is an adaptation for that assignment. Make a material adaptation explicit in brief production notes outside the publishable copy; respect the requested delivery location for those notes. Do not silently remove requested formats or silently relax a strict mode requirement. Resolve equally explicit incompatible requirements from context or ask for the unresolved choice while completing independent work.

Changing modes must preserve facts, stance, measurements, conditions, limitations, uncertainty, source destinations and protected text. Never import sample biography, anecdotes, opinions or results. Review both factual fidelity and the requested voice and presentation. These are writing choices, not a claim of author approval, measured imitation quality or equal reliability across models.
