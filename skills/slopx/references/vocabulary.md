# Vocabulary and phrase lists

These lists support rules P6 and P7. Use them to find sentences worth a second look. They are not ban lists.

How to use them:

- One listed word in a passage means nothing. Several in a paragraph usually mark a sentence that says little, and the fix is to rewrite the sentence around a fact (S2), since a synonym leaves it just as empty.
- Many of these are ordinary words that people use correctly. "Robust" is right for a statistical estimator. "Landscape" is right for a painting.
- The lists go out of date. Each generation of language models drops some habits and picks up others, so check the newest period first and review this file after major model releases.
- Older habits still turn up, because older and cheaper models stay in use.

## Words, by period

**2023 to mid-2024:** additionally (opening a sentence), bolstered, boasts, crucial, delve, enduring, garner, interplay, intricate, key, landscape, meticulous, pivotal, realm, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant

**Mid-2024 to mid-2025:** align with, enhance, fostering, highlighting, showcasing, underscore

**Mid-2025 to mid-2026:** emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing

**Mid-2026 onward:** challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally. These are very common words, so act only on a dense cluster.

**Coding assistants:** load-bearing, production-ready, comprehensive, robust, seamless, "Perfect!"

**Grok:** causal, empirical, correlate, underscore. These are normal in scientific writing, so act only on a cluster outside it.

**Business:** leverage, unlock, empower, elevate, seamless, game-changer, synergy, stakeholder alignment

## Phrases

**Inflated significance (S4):** stands as a testament to, serves as a reminder, plays a pivotal role, reflects a broader trend, setting the stage for, marks a significant shift, the evolving landscape of, leaves an indelible mark, deeply rooted in, rich history, nestled in the heart of, paving the way for, sheds light on

**Vague attribution (S5):** experts argue, observers have noted, industry reports suggest, studies show, critics say, has shaped discussions about, has been featured in several outlets

**Stock endings (S3):** despite these challenges, future outlook, in conclusion, in summary, overall, at the end of the day, only time will tell

**Chat leftovers (P7):** Certainly!, Of course!, Absolutely!, Great question, You're absolutely right, I hope this helps, Let me know if you'd like, Would you like me to, Is there anything else, Here is a comprehensive overview

**Empty emphasis (P7):** it's important to note that, it's worth noting that, when it comes to, here's the thing, let that sink in, the truth is

**Announced counts (P7):** here are five lessons, three reasons why, seven things I learned, several key takeaways. Keep the list if the items are real; drop the announcement.

**Clerk's voice (P7):** I appreciate your feedback, I have carefully reviewed, I apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused, if there are specific issues please point them out

**Disclaimers (P8):**

- 2023 to 2024: "It's important to note that regulations can vary depending on your jurisdiction."
- 2024 to 2025: "not publicly available", "not widely documented", "maintains a low profile", "based on available information"
- Mid-2026 onward: "should be treated as X rather than Y", "does not by itself establish", "should not be confused with"

## Things that are not signs of slop

Do not edit these on sight, and never cite them as evidence about who wrote a text:

- Correct grammar and spelling
- Formal or academic vocabulary in general
- A transition word on its own, such as "however" or "consequently"
- Hedges: can, may, might, tends to, perhaps
- Wordy but ordinary constructions: "in order to", "the fact that", "there is a"
- A mix of casual and formal register
- Em dashes in moderation, as a sign of who wrote a text (P10 still replaces them in an edit)
