# Fiction and roleplay

Fiction is the one place where invention is the job, so F1 works differently. You may propose concrete sensory detail, since the story's world belongs to the author and they can accept or change it. Say in the notes which details you added. Do not change plot events, who knows what, or anything the author has established about a character.

The author's style is the point. A writer who uses fragments, repetition or ornate description on purpose keeps them. Edit the stock phrase, and leave the deliberate choice.

## What to look for

**The first image anyone would reach for.** Stock detail tells the reader nothing about this room or this person. Replace it with something only this scene would contain.

**Feelings named in place of shown.** "A mix of fear and excitement" and "something she couldn't quite name" stand in for an action or a perception. Give the character something to do or notice.

**Narrator winks and summary.** "Little did she know", "maybe, just maybe", "for what felt like an eternity", and paragraph endings that explain what the scene meant.

**Tense drift.** A scene told in past tense that slips into present for a line or two (P11).

**The correction reflex.** "It wasn't fear, not exactly. It was something else entirely." State what it was (P3).

## Stock phrases

When several of these cluster in a passage, rewrite the passage:

- Body: "shivers down her spine", "swallowed hard", "breath hitched", "heart hammered against his ribs", "knuckles whitening", "took a deep breath", "let out a breath she didn't know she was holding"
- Voice: "barely above a whisper", "her voice dripping with", "eyes sparkling with mischief"
- Atmosphere: "casting long shadows", "the air hung thick", "the air was heavy with the scent of", "words hung in the air", "ethereal glow", "palpable tension", "a symphony of"
- Emotion: "a mix of X and Y", "couldn't help but", "torn between", "couldn't shake the feeling", "the weight of unspoken"
- Time: "for the first time in years", "one last time"

Human genre fiction uses some of these too. One in a chapter is no problem.

## Names

Language models reach for the same names when none is given: Elara, Mara, Elias, Kael, Kaelen, Lyra, Eira, Seraphina, Thorne, Voss, Blackwood, Vance. Settings repeat too: the lighthouse, the clockmaker, the keeper.

If a draft uses several of these and the author has not said they chose them, mention it in the notes and let the author rename. Do not rename characters yourself, because a name is a protected fact of the story (F2).

## Openings

Check the first paragraph against the obvious: the year stated in the first clause, weather, a character waking, a sensory inventory of a room. Suggest starting where something changes.

## Example

Before:

> Elara's heart hammered against her ribs as she pushed open the door. The air hung thick with the scent of old parchment, and the lighthouse keeper's words echoed in her mind. It wasn't fear, not exactly. It was something else entirely.

After:

> Elara shouldered the door until the swollen wood gave. Inside it smelled of mouse and kerosene. Her uncle had said the logbook was under the stairs, which meant he had never been up here.

Notes for this edit would say that the mouse, the kerosene, the uncle and the logbook are proposed details, and that "Elara" is a name models overuse.
