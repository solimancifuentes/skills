# Social posts and comments

Readers scroll past posts that could have been written by anyone about anything. The test for a post: does it contain one thing the reader can check or one thing that happened to the author? A name, a number, a date, a place or a cost will do. If it has none and the user cannot supply one, say so (F1). Do not invent an anecdote.

A second test: could a reasonable person disagree with it? If not, the post has no position. Ask the author what they think.

## Patterns to remove

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Stock opener | "Thrilled to announce", "I'm humbled to share", "I've been thinking a lot about", "In today's fast-paced world" |
| Hollow hook | "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "Read that again", "The result?", "Here's what nobody tells you", "Let's dive in" |
| The flip | "That's not failure. That's feedback." (P3) |
| Story bait | "A 5-year-old taught me about leadership." |
| Slogan triad | "Faster. Smarter. Better." (P4) |
| One sentence per line | Every sentence set as its own paragraph (T1) |
| Moral plus bait | "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment YES", "Repost if you agree", "Tag someone who needs this", "Follow for more", "Bookmark this" |
| Decoration | Emoji or arrow bullets, four or more hashtags (T2) |
| Filler closer | "At the end of the day..." |
| No specifics | "a major client", "a recent experience" |

## Shape

- Social posts are plain text. Remove Markdown, and use straight quotation marks (T2, P10).
- No colons or semicolons as prose punctuation in posts, comments, replies or their drafts (P10). Split the sentence or join the clauses with a comma and a conjunction. "The lesson: ship smaller" becomes "The lesson was to ship smaller." "We missed the date; procurement took 11 weeks" becomes "We missed the date because procurement took 11 weeks." Colons in times (9:30), ratios (3:1), URLs, code and quotations stay.
- Write paragraphs. A line break is for a change of subject.
- A real question at the end is fine when the author wants the answer.
- Keep the author's own turns of phrase. The aim is their post with the filler gone, in their voice.

## Comments

A comment that only summarizes the post it replies to adds nothing. It should add a fact, a disagreement, an example from the commenter's experience or a question. If the draft has none of these, tell the user.

A reply to criticism should answer the point. Cut the clerk's voice ("I appreciate your feedback and have carefully reviewed", "I apologize for any inconvenience", "please point out the specific issues") and say what the author will change or why they disagree.

A congratulation needs no specifics, but it should not carry stock add-ons such as "Can't wait to see what's next for you!" or "Onwards and upwards!" padding a line that already says it. Cut those and keep it short. Do not turn it into a template with slots for the user to fill.

When the user asks for comments to be less generic, a comment that only agrees ("So true!", "100% this") should lose the agreement opener even if you cannot add a detail.

## Example

Before:

> I almost quit last year.
>
> Here's the thing.
>
> It's not about working harder. It's about working smarter.
>
> Focus. Discipline. Consistency.
>
> Agree?

Nothing here can be checked, so the edit depends on the author. Ask what happened. With their facts:

> In May we lost a contract worth a fifth of our bookings because our security review took 11 weeks. We now send our SOC 2 report on the first call, and reviews take about 3 weeks. Has anyone found a way to shorten procurement further?
