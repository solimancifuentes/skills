# Work writing: emails, status updates, reports, memos

The reader is busy and will act on what you send. Workplace slop looks finished and moves nothing forward: the recipient has to work out what happened, check it, and often redo it.

## What the reader needs

A status update or report should let the reader answer these without writing back:

- What happened and when, with the number behind any result the draft claims.
- Who owns each item.
- What is late or at risk, and why.
- What decision or action is needed, from whom, by when.

If the draft cannot answer one of these and the answer is not in what the user gave you, that is an F1 gap. Do not fill it. Cut the sentence that only gestures at it and list the gap in the notes, so the user can send the result as it is or add the facts first. A short email with two real facts is a good result.

## Patterns to remove

- Openers that say nothing: "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to provide a comprehensive update", "I'm reaching out to".
- Progress with no content: "significant progress across multiple workstreams", "continued momentum", "enhanced stakeholder alignment", "optimized processes", "drive impactful outcomes".
- Closers that commit to nothing: "Moving forward, we will continue to monitor and iterate as needed", "Please don't hesitate to reach out".
- A report with no name, number or date in a hundred words. That is the clearest sign nothing has been said (S2).
- Unfilled placeholders: `[Name]`, `[Company]`, `[Date]` (F4).
- Cover letters and reviews written from a template: "I am keen to", "I recently had the pleasure of".

## Shape

- Put the request or the decision in the first two lines. Background follows.
- An email body is plain text. No Markdown headings or bold (T2).
- A short list is fine for parallel items such as three dated milestones. An explanation of why something slipped is a paragraph (T1).
- Match length to content. Three facts need three sentences.

## Example

Before:

> Hi team, I wanted to provide a comprehensive update on Q3 initiatives. Overall, we have made significant progress across multiple workstreams, leveraging synergies to drive impactful outcomes. Moving forward, we will continue to monitor KPIs and iterate as needed.

There is nothing here to edit into shape, and nothing to send. Say so in one line, list what the update needs (what shipped and when, what slipped and why, any decision the team must make and by when), and ask for it. If the user asked for a template to fill in, return the skeleton with markers:

> Q3 update: [need: what shipped, with dates] [need: what slipped and why] [need: any decision you need from the team, and by when]

When the draft has some facts, keep them and cut the rest. "The billing migration finished on June 3. Priya is waiting on legal's review of the new terms." is a complete email. The notes can ask when legal expects to finish, because the team may need to plan around it. They should not ask for migration metrics, because the draft claims no result.

If the user supplies the facts, the result looks like this:

> Q3: the billing migration finished on June 3 (Priya). Failed invoices fell from 1.8% to 0.6%. The refund-flow work slipped to Oct 30 because the payments API changed. Legal expects to finish reviewing the new terms by Friday.
