# Academic writing: papers, abstracts, peer reviews, citations

Readers of a paper check claims against evidence. The edit must leave every result, citation and qualification exactly as strong as the author made it.

## Protect more here

- Every number with its unit, sample size, confidence interval and p-value.
- Every citation key, DOI, author name and year.
- Hedges that state the strength of evidence: "suggests", "is associated with", "in this sample". These are claims, and removing one changes the finding (F3).
- Terms of art. One term per concept is already the convention (P5), and a "plainer" synonym may be a different concept.

## Patterns to remove

- Abstract openers that announce: "This article delves into", "In recent years, there has been growing interest in".
- Significance with no content: "sheds light on", "paves the way for", "a crucial step towards", "has garnered significant attention" (S4).
- Hype adjectives with no measurement behind them: "groundbreaking", "novel" used more than once, "remarkable".
- Closing paragraphs on "challenges and future prospects" that name no specific challenge or experiment.
- Vague attribution: "studies have shown" with no citation (S5). If the citation is missing, mark `[need: citation]`. Do not supply one from memory (F1).
- Chat leftovers pasted into the manuscript: "regenerate response", "as an AI language model", "as of my last knowledge update" (F4).

Formal register is correct here. Leave it.

## Citations

Report a citation that looks wrong and do not fix it by guessing:

- A DOI or link that does not resolve, or resolves to a different title.
- A reference listed but never cited in the text, or cited and not listed.
- A book citation with no page number where the claim is specific.
- A tracking parameter in a URL, such as `utm_source=chatgpt.com`. Remove the parameter and tell the author, since it shows where the link came from.

## Peer reviews

A review is useful when the authors can act on it. Each criticism should name the section, table or claim it concerns, and say what would resolve it.

Remove:

- Praise that fits any paper: "well-written and timely", "makes a valuable contribution".
- Requests with no object: "the authors should strengthen the evaluation".
- Suggested citations the reviewer has not read. Mark them `[need: confirm this paper exists and is relevant]`.

If a review draft contains no point specific to the paper, say so in the notes. Do not write one for the user.
