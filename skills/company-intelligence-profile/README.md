# Company intelligence profile

Research a company or compare a small set of companies in concise profiles that cite their sources and fit the business being studied.

## Installation

```sh
npx skills add solimancifuentes/skills --skill company-intelligence-profile
```

## Process

1. Resolve the name, domain, ticker, or profile URL to a specific company. Clarify ambiguity when choosing the wrong entity would change the answer.
2. Verify its status at the reporting date. A public company, family business, subsidiary, and nonprofit need different report sections and sources.
3. Research the relevant questions using primary sources first. Date financial figures, label estimates, and stop chasing a likely private metric after one or two reasonable attempts.
4. Write the profile inline, with sources and brief notes on expected information that the reviewed sources did not establish.

## Shape the research

Provide a company identifier and your purpose. Ask for a quick snapshot, a standard profile, deeper research, or a focus on sales, recruiting, investment-relevant information, or public markets. You can also specify an as-of date, source restrictions, or particular questions.

The report adapts to the evidence. It does not assume every company is a startup, borrow a parent's stock figures for a subsidiary, or invent private revenue and valuation data.

## What you get

Profiles cover supported details about the business, products, leadership, finances, competitors, and risks. Comparisons add a short table. Facts, estimates, and sales hypotheses remain distinct; missing figures do not become rows of filler.

Output stays in the conversation unless you request a file. Investment-related profiles provide information and open questions, without buy/sell recommendations. Broad due diligence is outside this workflow.
