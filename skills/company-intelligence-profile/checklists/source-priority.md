# Source Priority Checklist

Use the relevant source order for the claim and category. Stop when a section is credibly filled — the goal is reliability per token, not exhaustiveness. Label aggregator numbers as estimates; date every figure.

## Public companies

1. Company investor relations pages
2. SEC/EDGAR or equivalent securities regulator
3. Stock exchange listing pages
4. Earnings releases
5. Annual reports (10-K / 20-F / equivalent)
6. Quarterly reports (10-Q / equivalent)
7. Reliable finance data providers (for current price/market cap)
8. Reputable financial news

## Recently IPO'd companies

Same as public, plus: S-1/prospectus for pre-IPO financials and risk factors; IPO announcement for offer price/exchange; finance data for current-vs-IPO price. Mention lockup/post-IPO dynamics only if publicly discussed and relevant.

## Private companies & startups

- **Funding, rounds, and valuation:** Company funding announcements and relevant public filings → investor announcements and portfolio pages → reputable news → accessible Crunchbase / Dealroom / PitchBook / CB Insights / Tracxn-style sources. Check available direct announcements before accepting an aggregator's funding figure; retain attribution and explain material conflicts briefly.
- **Products, business model, and pricing:** Company product documentation, pricing pages, and website → reputable coverage → app stores, review sites, and traffic estimates as secondary signals.
- **Leadership:** Follow the Executives & people order below, starting with the official leadership page.
- **Hiring and team signals:** Company careers pages and job postings → LinkedIn company profile or relevant professional profiles → other attributed secondary sources. Keep headcount estimates labeled as estimates.

An aggregator may help discover an announcement; its position as a discovery tool does not make it the preferred evidence. Apply the existing 1–2-attempt limit to metrics likely to be non-public, and describe unsuccessful searches as gaps in the sources reviewed.

## Acquired companies

1. Acquirer press release
2. Target company announcement
3. SEC filings if the acquirer is public (8-K, merger docs — often the only place deal terms appear)
4. Reputable news coverage
5. Company website / parent website (current brand/integration status)

## Nonprofits / foundations

1. Organization website (mission, programs, leadership)
2. Public filings (Form 990 in the US, charity regulator equivalents elsewhere) — best source for revenue/assets/compensation
3. Annual/impact reports
4. Grant databases and major-donor announcements
5. Reputable news coverage

## Executives & people

1. Company leadership page
2. SEC filings if public (proxy statements name officers/comp)
3. Official announcements (appointments/departures)
4. LinkedIn or professional profiles
5. Reputable interviews/news

## Cross-cutting rules

- Primary/official beats aggregated/secondary. If a number appears only on aggregators, attribute it ("per Crunchbase") and treat as estimate.
- One confirmation from a top-tier source is enough; don't triple-verify routine facts.
- If two credible sources disagree, report both in one clause with dates.
- "Only public sources" / "no paid databases" constraints: skip gated databases entirely and say which class of source was skipped.
- Marketing pages are valid for product/positioning, NOT for financial facts.
- For anything volatile (stock price, market cap, headcount, executive roster), prefer the freshest source and date-stamp it.
