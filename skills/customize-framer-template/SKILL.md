# Customize Framer Template

## Outcome

Customize the existing project using its components, styles, and responsive behavior as the starting point. Ground new content in the business's facts, and choose checks that address the actual impact of each change.

## Use the available Framer environment

- For live project work, use an authorized Framer connection or editor and its supported setup, session, API, recovery, and publishing procedures. Verify capabilities against the current environment and official Framer documentation when they matter.
- Confirm the exact project before live inspection or mutation. Stop if the verified identity does not match the user's target.
- Use component-authoring and image-generation capabilities only when the requested work needs them. Respect Framer's component, layout, and asset constraints, and the chosen tool's setup and data-handling requirements.
- When installed, integrations such as `framer`, `framer-code-components`, or `imagegen` can supply those capabilities. They are optional implementations, not prerequisites for this skill. Follow their applicable entry points without expanding the user's grant.

Planning, factual briefing, content mapping, and prompt drafting do not require a live Framer connection. Report unavailable live checks honestly and continue work supported by supplied material. Skill selection follows the user's and host's invocation settings; selecting a skill does not itself authorize setup, installation, or project changes.

## Match authority to effects

Treat the current request and still-applicable grants in the conversation as the authority envelope.

- A read-only request authorizes no mutation.
- A change request may authorize routine reversible work directly, including choosing copy or fit adjustments when the requested outcome leaves those choices open. Resolve target identity through inspection; the user need not supply every layer ID or final value.
- A broad customization request can cover ordinary content, style, and asset work needed for the stated outcome. Clarify only material ambiguity, unsupported facts, or effects beyond that request.
- Approval follows covered effects, not phase numbers. A copy-only request does not imply style, imagery, structure, data handling, external-service, deletion, or publication authority.
- One coherent approval may cover the enumerated mutation, focused validation, a bounded same-scope corrective pass, and the corresponding tracker update.
- Do not ask for another approval merely to complete validation, record semantic success, normalize an equivalent platform representation, or inspect another related read-only item already inside scope.

Split mixed scopes or handle them at their highest effect class. Continue through work already inside the envelope; stop only when a new material decision or effect is required.

### Read-only work

Inspection, inventory, mapping, audit, and recommendation without mutation are read-only. The request authorizes related discovery within its named boundary, and related units may be batched.

### Routine reversible work

Page-local copy, links, existing properties, local visibility, and asset-slot changes with limited impact are routine reversible work. The existing request or an approved map is sufficient when it covers the outcome and effects. Apply, validate, reconcile only within scope, update an authorized tracker if applicable, and report.

### Guarded work

Shared components, multiple consumers, CMS values, reusable styles, responsive structure, and meaningful hierarchy additions are guarded. Identify affected surfaces and check that the existing grant covers their effects. Ask only when that inspection reveals new material scope or a decision reserved for the user. Use a recovery path proportional to the impact.

### Consequential work

Publication, external services or data, analytics, domains, account settings, deletion, detachment, system replacement, CMS schema or binding changes, and other difficult-to-reverse effects need authority that covers those effects. Use any still-applicable explicit grant; prior inspection, review, or generic implementation approval alone is insufficient.

## Preserve the template and factual integrity

Treat the existing hierarchy, reusable components and instances, visual tokens, responsive behavior, interactions, imagery, and CMS bindings as the default specification. Do not redesign, detach, replace a system, or alter an unapproved surface merely to make an approved change easier.

Use only user-supplied facts or facts verified from approved sources. Do not invent claims, proof, customers, testimonials, logos, metrics, results, awards, credentials, integrations, pricing, locations, legal conclusions, or other business facts. Mark missing inputs plainly. Keep product interfaces, identity, certifications, and customer evidence authentic. Generated imagery must not impersonate authentic interfaces, identity, certifications, or customer proof.

Review and validation never imply publication. Never describe a proposal, draft, hidden object, queued operation, branch, or preview as published.

## Fit content without silently changing intent

Preserve wording the user has fixed. Use fit adjustments within scope, such as permitted wrapping or auto-height, and ask only when fitting it requires a material choice outside the grant. When the user has delegated copywriting, write and apply compatible copy from verified facts without a separate proposal gate. Preserve intended meaning, tone, and protected styles.

## Treat hiding, additions, and cleanup proportionately

Reversible hiding is the default. An approved map may include enumerated hides and small reuse-based additions without artificial sub-gates. For each affected object, account for known consumers and validate layout collapse, interaction and focus behavior, accessibility exposure, navigation, indexing, CMS behavior, and materially affected responsive states as applicable. Do not claim accessibility-tree validation from Canvas visibility alone.

Classify a page, section, reusable component, CMS object, schema change, or multi-object hierarchy by its actual effects. For an addition, identify its purpose, placement, reused template element, required content or asset, proof dependency, responsive and accessibility effects, and navigation, CMS, or SEO effects. Prefer the smallest viable reuse-based addition.

Permanent deletion or detachment requires exact targets, consumer and dependency checks, verified recovery, and explicit current authorization. A target with any live consumer or dependency remains blocked; delete only a target proven unused. Do not substitute deletion when only hiding was authorized.

For identity, inventory, factual briefing, copy, shared surfaces, or CMS content, read [references/content-workflow.md](references/content-workflow.md).

For colors, typography, imagery, SEO, launch review, or publishing, read [references/design-and-launch.md](references/design-and-launch.md).

For complete customization, use the relevant modules in dependency order without forcing optional phases or restarting completed work. For a partial request, load only the relevant references and begin from verified current state.

## Track multi-unit work compactly

Create or use a tracker only for multi-unit customization or when the user or project already requires one. Never create a local tracking file without write authority. Track separate axes:

```text
decision:
  undecided | keep | change | hide | defer | exclude

implementation:
  uninspected | mapped | approved | applied | validated | not applicable

activation:
  ready | blocked by named trigger | active | unverified | not applicable

publication:
  unpublished | draft | published | unverified | not applicable
```

Store external dependencies as refresh triggers, not copied dossiers of another system's mutable state. Update changed rows once per run; do not reproduce the full tracker in every report. Keep private briefs and raw project evidence in the authorized workspace. Shared reports should omit account details, access-bearing URLs, customer data, and unrelated source paths.

## Report material outcomes

Report the authorized scope, material proposals or changes, validation actually performed, deviations or incidents, unresolved decisions, manual or unverified checks, activation and publication state, and the next consequential gate. Distinguish proposed, approved, applied, validated, published, and manual states. Omit empty boilerplate and unchanged tracker rows. Disclose sampling instead of implying exhaustive coverage.

## Stop only for material reasons

Before mutation, refresh the exact project and scoped targets. Stop on project mismatch, material target drift, missing authority, or expanded impact.

Never retry an uncertain write blindly. Inspect effective state first.

A bounded corrective pass is allowed only when:

- the project and target still match;
- the approved outcome is unchanged;
- partial state is observable and unambiguous;
- the cause is understood;
- the remaining correction affects only authorized targets or enumerated overrides;
- the correction is reversible;
- it introduces no new object, dependency, external action, destructive action, publication effect, or higher consequence class; and
- immediate focused readback can prove the final and protected states.

Stop when partial state is unknown, recovery is uncertain, scope expands, a material visual or accessibility problem persists, or consequential authority is absent.

Do not stop solely because semantically equivalent serialization differs, inherited values lack local materialization, computed geometry reflows without an authored change, or a transient warning is absent from the final state.
